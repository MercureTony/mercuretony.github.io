*"Too much of the work is still manual."*

That's the sentence on the screen behind me (Figure 1). It's also the simplest way to explain what [Coalesc](https://coalesc.ai) is for.

![Anthony on stage in front of a slide that reads "Too much of the work is still manual," listing collect documents, figure out what's missing, check the numbers, move information between systems and prepare the file for review, with a seated audience in the foreground.](/writings/why-im-building-coalesc/stage.jpg "Presenting at Canada Fintech Forum in Montréal, September 15, 2026.")

Every accounting firm employs people to do work that software will increasingly be able to do. Coalesc is building that software.

## Two milestones

Over the past months, I've spent a lot of time inside accounting firms, walking through real files with partners and their teams. One thing stood out early: receiving a client's documents and having a file ready for review are two very different milestones. Between them sits an enormous amount of work that rarely gets discussed outside the profession.

Documents arrive over weeks, across several emails. Someone has to sort them, work out what's still missing and chase the client. A return can sit for days waiting on a single answer. A preparer starts a file, discovers a gap halfway through, sets it aside, and later has to rebuild their understanding before continuing. When the file finally reaches review, one correction can send it back: a section to redo, taxes to recalculate, and the return, the provision and the financial statements to bring back in line.

At the end of all that, a surprisingly hard question remains: is this file actually ready for review?

Much of this work is necessary. Some of it requires real professional judgment. But a significant portion is repetitive preparation that shouldn't need to be done by hand, again, every year.

## What we're building

Coalesc is building the execution layer between client intake and accountant review. It gathers and organizes what the client sends, identifies what's missing and follows up, enters and checks the numbers, and hands the accountant a file that is ready to review, with the evidence behind every number, the questions still open and the exceptions that need a person.

Accountants keep the part they trained for: reviewing results and making the decisions that require their expertise.

It's already changing the arithmetic. One of our customers used to hire a full-time seasonal employee every tax season. Last season, Coalesc saved them more than 85 hours of work in two months, on a single workflow: getting tax returns to clients for sign-off.

## Bigger than paperwork

This is a much bigger opportunity than automating paperwork.

Accounting firms have always grown the same way: more clients means more staff. Every new engagement brings more preparation, more follow-up and more review cycles. Capacity has meant headcount.

If software can reliably handle a growing share of that work, the equation changes. A firm can serve significantly more clients without hiring proportionally. Its people spend their days on review, advice and decisions instead of assembling files. That's a different kind of firm, and I think it's where the profession is going.

## Why reliability is the whole game

What partners have told me, again and again, is that speed isn't enough. Their names are on the work. Thousands of files and decades of client trust depend on it being correct, complete and traceable. Software that does the work has to show its work.

So we hold ourselves to a simple test: how much of what we prepare can an accountant accept as it is, and how much do they still have to fix or redo? Every point we move on that measure is capacity a firm gets back. That's why we build on real files with accounting teams, starting with tax, compilation and year-end work. The details that break a clean demo are the ones that matter.

## Where this goes

Today, firms grow by hiring. I believe they'll grow by serving more clients with the people they have, with software doing the preparation behind every engagement and accountants spending their time on the work only they can do. If that's right, the economics of running an accounting firm change.

That's what I'm spending my time on.
